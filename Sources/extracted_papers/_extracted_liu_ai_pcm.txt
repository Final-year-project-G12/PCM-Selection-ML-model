ABSTRACT
Artificial Intelligence (AI) is leading the charge in revolutionizing research methodologies within the field of 
latent heat storage (LHS) by using phase change materials (PCMs) and elevating their overall efficiency. This 
comprehensive review delves into AI applications within the domain of PCM for TES systems, mainly including 
prediction and optimization. The review article emphasizes the crucial role of AI in predicting physical properties 
of composite PCM and its performance in LHS systems. Also, the review article highlights the significance of AI in 
optimizing the structure and layout, as well as the operation and control strategies of latent heat storage systems 
using PCMs across various research fields. The study at hand discusses literature encompassing both experi -
mental and theoretical articles that detail the integration of AI techniques within TES systems by using PCM, and 
compares the advantages and limitations of AI prediction models and optimization algorithms with existing 
typical technologies in the field of LHS. The summarization of the limitations in prior research has been pre -
sented, along with the proposal of potential avenues for performance enhancement of AI applied in LHS system. 
Additionally, the primary directions and challenges for future investigations have been emphasized, accompa -
nied by suggested strategies.

2. Overview of the application of AI in PCM energy storage
Experiments and numerical simulations are the prevailing research 
methodologies employed in the field of PCM energy storage, as evi -
denced by previous studies [ 40 , 41 ]. These approaches primarily aim to 
investigate the thermal properties of diverse composite phase change 
materials (CPCM) [ 25 , 42 ], as along with the dynamic characteristics 
and thermodynamic performance of theses CPCM in LHS units and 
systems [ 43 ]. The utilization of these two conventional methodologies 
has significantly facilitated the identification of advanced PCMs and the 
implementation of energy storage systems. However, it has been pro -
gressively ascertained that the exploration of phase change energy 
storage laws, encompassing experimental design and construction, as 
well as the establishment and computation of physical and mathemat -
ical models, has entailed substantial time and financial resources 
through experiments and numerical investigations. Notably, the heat 
transfer model of PCMs entails intricate partial differential equations 
[ 44 ]. Over the past two decades, the rapid advancement of ML and deep 
learning technology has led to an increasing need for the integration of 
AI tools as partial substitutes for certain traditional research method -
ologies [ 45 , 46 ].
Fig. 3 illustrates the primary applications of AI technology in PCM 
energy storage, encompassing solar thermal utilization, electronic de -
vice thermal management, and building energy conservation. In 
essence, these applications can be categorized into two overarching 
functions: prediction and optimization. Concerning prediction, ML al -
gorithms are commonly employed to investigate the physical properties, 
phase transition behavior, and thermal performance of LHS units and 
PCM systems. In terms of optimization, advanced numerical optimiza -
tion algorithms based on AI are used to investigate the physical prop -
erties of PCMs, the configuration of LHS, as well as the boundary 
conditions and operating strategies of the system. Therefore, this review 
will focus on analyzing the applications of AI technology in these two 
areas.
3. AI for prediction in PCM energy storage
3.1. Physical properties of PCM
Currently, AI is extensively employed for the prediction of the 
physical characteristics of composite CPCMs. The primary objective 
behind the development of CPCMs typically revolves around addressing 
the inherent physical deficiencies observed in pure PCMs, including the 
inadequate thermal conductivity of organic PCMs and the elevated 
melting point or reduced decomposition temperature of solar molten 
salts. These limitations associated with pure PCM significantly impede 
their practical applicability. Furthermore, the integration of PCM with 
various other materials has been shown to significantly enhance their 
overall performance, leading to increased utilization in specific domains 
[ 47 ]. A comprehensive analysis of recent scholarly publications reveals 
that AI predictions have successfully categorized the physical attributes 
of CPCMs into three distinct categories: high-temperature composite 
molten salts for solar thermal power plants, PCM-based composites for 
improved thermal conductivity, and low melting point PCM-concrete 
composites for building energy conservation in construction 
applications.
In the field of CPCM prediction, ML stands out as the predominant AI 
technique employed. Typically, these ML algorithms necessitate input 
and output variables for both model training and data prediction. The 
input variables typically comprise crucial parameters that exert an in -
fluence on the material’s physical properties, while the output param -
eters generally represent the parameters to be predicted. Hence, the 
establishment of a correspondence between output variables and input 
variables can be accomplished by utilizing the classification and 
regression functions inherent in diverse ML models or algorithms. The 
architectural depiction of the ML model for forecasting CPCMs is

Phase diagram technology is utilized as an additional approach for 
forecasting the properties of molten salts. These diagrams are instru -
mental in illustrating the equilibrium liquid phase lines of molten salts, 
thereby facilitating the examination of various properties such as 
melting point, specific heat capacity, solubility, and eutectic composi -
tion [ 60 , 61 ]. Historically, phase diagrams of molten salt have predom -
inantly been constructed through experimental measurement of liquidus 
lines. Nonetheless, this method is limited in its ability to provide precise 
and comprehensive phase diagrams, while also necessitating extensive 
time and labor for data collection. In the contemporary age character -
ized by rapid advancements in computer technology, the phase diagram 
thermodynamic calculation, commonly referred to as the CALPHAD 
method, has emerged as a novel approach to the development of phase 
diagrams [ 62 ]. This method leverages thermodynamic principles to 
elucidate the diverse thermodynamic characteristics of a material sys -
tem across different phase states within the realm of phase equilibrium. 
Fundamentally, the CALPHAD method aims to construct phase equi -
librium and phase diagrams based on empirical data. The CALPHAD 
method offers the advantage of extrapolating binary system phase dia -
grams to calculate ternary systems, yet its predictive capabilities are 
typically limited to melting point, specific heat capacity, and eutectic 
composition [ 63 ]. In the realm of energy storage, AI technology has 
demonstrated superior accuracy in predicting latent heat. In the most 
recent study [ 64 ], AI-generated data was utilized as the primary source 
for developing phase diagrams through the CALPHAD method. The 
integration of the CALPHAD approach with AI prediction technology is 
employed to enhance prediction efficiency and precision.

3.1.3. PCM integrated cement-based concrete
The incorporation of PCMs or microencapsulated PCMs (MPCMs) 
into concrete offers advantages in terms of temperature regulation and 
energy consumption reduction in buildings [ 75 , 76 ]. However, it is 
commonly observed that this integration tends to decrease the 
compressive strength of the concrete [ 77 , 78 ]. Several studies have 
demonstrated that the thermal properties and mechanical strength of 
PCM-concrete based on cement are influenced by a multitude of pa -
rameters, thus rendering the relationship complex. In addition to the 
thermal properties of PCM, it also includes the softness of MPCM par -
ticles, the porosity of concrete caused by MPCM, the damage of micro -
capsule shells during concrete mixing and pouring, and the load on 
hardened concrete [ 76 , 79 , 80 ]. The highly nonlinear effects of these 
parameters on the thermal properties and mechanical strength make ML 
a promising technology for predicting the performance of PCM inte -
grated cement-based concrete.
In terms of predicting compressive strength, the input variables 
typically included phase transition temperature, latent heat, density, 
and dose of PCM. In addition, it should also include cement, water, fine/ 
coarse aggregate, superplasticizer, age, and curing temperature. Afshin 
Marani et al. [ 81 ] used ML regression algorithms including random 
forests, additional trees, gradient boosting, and extreme gradient 
boosting to predict the compressive strength of PCM integrated 
cement-based composites. The flow chart is shown as Fig. 7 (a). The 
results indicated that the gradient boosting model had the highest ac -
curacy, and the content of fine aggregate and PCM microcapsules had a 
more significant impact on the predicted compressive strength, as it 
affects the particle packing density in the cementitious matrix. Besides, 
the age of the test sample and the thermal performance of PCMs 
(including melting temperature and latent heat) also had a notable 
impact on the predicted output values. As shown in Fig. 7 (b), Harun 
et al. [ 82 ] proposed a DL algorithm that combines extreme learning 
machines (ELM) with automatic encoders to successfully predict the 
compressive strength of PCM-concrete and conducted a sensitivity 
analysis. This model improved the prediction accuracy of DL and ach -
ieved ideal model generalization ability. The research table details that 
aggregates have the greatest impact on compressive strength, followed 
by cement and water content.
In another study [ 83 ], the authors adopted the same number of 
experimental datasets and input variables as the previous study [ 82 ] to 
predict the compressive strength of concrete mixed with MPCM. The 
results suggested that the amount of cement and the density of MPCM 
have a significant impact on the compressive strength of concrete. 
Furthermore, the mass ratio of PCM to cement (PCM/C) was highly 
correlated with compressive strength, and lower ratios achieved higher 
target compressive strength. Another contribution of this study is the 
creation of a large synthetic dataset using Generative Adversarial Net -
works (TGANs) and the training of the synthetic data using ML models 
including Random Forest Regression (RFR), Gradient Augmented 
Regression (GBR), and Deep Neural Networks (DNN). This work dem -
onstrates the feasibility of developing synthetic data to train 
high-precision ML prediction models when available data is insufficient.
In addition to focusing on compressive strength, recent studies 
focused on the prediction of freeze-thaw cycles (FTCs) [ 84 ] and 
apparent activation energies [ 85 ] of PCM-concrete. Li et al. [ 84 ] utilized 
a neural network algorithm to provide a reliable continuous mapping 
between design parameters and the number of FTCs. The input variables 
considered include the thickness of the protective PCM-concrete layer, 
the diameter, volume fraction, phase transition temperature, and latent 
heat of the MPCM. According to the results, the layer ’ s thickness appears 
to be the most significant factor influencing the FTC ’ s number, followed 
by the volume fraction of PCM and phase transition temperature. Afshin 
et al. [ 85 ] showed that DNNs outperform gradient enhanced ensemble in 
predicting the hydration rate and accumulated heat of cement-based 
composite PCM. The predicted data is further used to calculate the 
apparent activation energy. The predicted results indicated that the 
addition of 10 wt% and 20 wt% MPCM to cement reduces the apparent 
activation energy by nearly 10 % and 28 %, respectively, indicating that 
the hydration reaction is less sensitive to temperature.

3.2.2. Buildings integrated with PCM
Due to the significant contribution of building energy consumption 
to overall energy consumption [ 107 , 108 ], the integration of PCMs in 
buildings holds promise for mitigating peak energy demand, regulating 
room temperature, enhancing thermal comfort, and partially reducing 
reliance on air conditioning systems [ 109 ]. The incorporation of PCM in 
building integration encompasses both passive and active approaches. In 
passive structures [ 110–112 ], PCM is integrated into the building en -
velope or prepared as a modular structure within the interior. Despite 
the space-saving benefits offered by the passive structure, it also pre -
sents significant challenges in terms of leakage, mechanical properties, 
and fire resistance of PCM. In contrast, active structures typically 
incorporate PCM into a distinct LHS unit, in conjunction with ventilation 
and heating systems [ 113 , 114 ]. However, it is important to note that 
active systems may decrease building safety requirements but result in 
larger system dimensions. In academic literature concerning the pre -
diction of thermal performance in building integrated PCM through the 
use of AI technique, the focus is primarily on passive structures. Fig. 8
illustrates the integration of PCM within building walls, interiors, roofs, 
and light earth. The thermal performance of PCM-integrated building 
envelope structures is primarily influenced by various parameters, 
including climate conditions, PCM layer thickness, relative positioning 
of PCM layers, and the thermal physical properties of the PCM. These

4.1. Optimization of structure and layout
4.1.1. Solar thermal utilization integrated LHS unit
The incorporation of LHS into energy systems frequently yields un -
foreseen benefits, particularly in the context of solar energy utilization 
[ 40 ]. The integration of LHS in such systems contributes to the consis -
tent and reliable operation of the system, ultimately enhancing its reli -
ability and economic viability. Table 3 provides an overview of research 
endeavors focused on optimizing solar system integration with LHS 
units. These investigations encompassed the integration of LHS into 
various systems, including solar absorption chillers [ 132 , 133 ], solar 
power [ 134 , 135 ], and solar domestic hot water [ 136 ] systems.
The thermodynamic performance of LHS plays a vital role in the 
overall system performance, necessitating the implementation of 
advanced optimization techniques to enhance heat transfer efficiency. 
Among these techniques, GA was widely employed as an optimization 
method. GA are particularly well-suited for addressing complex and 
high-dimensional problems, often in conjunction with mathematical 
models or computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations.
In the context of optimization, the structural parameters and pipeline 
layout of LHS heat exchangers were commonly considered as variables 
for optimization, with the objective function typically aimed at 
enhancing the thermodynamic performance or reducing the operational 
expenses of the system. GA is capable of addressing multi-objective 
optimization problems as well. In a particular investigation [ 132 ], the 
system ’ s investment costs were minimized while simultaneously maxi -
mizing the coefficient of performance (COP) for the multi-objective 
optimization of the system. The findings of the optimization analysis 
indicated a 3.89 % enhancement in the coefficient of performance (COP) 
of the system, accompanied by a 9.04 % reduction in investment costs. 
Nevertheless, it is important to note that the optimization variables 
examined in this study do not pertain to the LHS unit, thereby raising 
concerns regarding the extent to which the LHS structure influences the 
overall system performance.
Additionally, the efficacy of the optimization outcomes in diverse 
boundary settings or operational circumstances warrants further inves -
tigation. In the study conducted by Lu et al. [ 136 ], the optimization of a 
solar household hot water system with an integrated multi-pipe PCM 
heat exchanger was examined. The system ’ s performance was evaluated 
under the boundary condition of an HTF flow rate of 1.68 kg/min. The 
results indicated that the pipeline layout, when optimized, exhibited 
enhanced performance at the flow rate of 1.68 kg/min. However, it 
demonstrated a decline in performance when the flow rate was reduced 
to 0.5 kg/min. This suggests that the outcomes of optimization in spe -
cific operational circumstances may not be universally applicable to 
other operational circumstances, particularly in the case of complex 
systems comprising multiple components, as their performance is 
greatly influenced by boundary conditions. Regrettably, limited 
research has been dedicated to addressing the issue of generalizing 
optimization outcomes.

4.1.3. Geometrical parameter of fins integrated into the LHS unit
Fin technology has been demonstrated to be an effective and cost- 
efficient approach for addressing the issue of low thermal conductivity 
in PCM [ 140 ]. Fins are commonly employed in various configurations of 
LHS units, such as shell-and-tube, rectangular, and spherical structures 
[ 141 ]. Furthermore, the arrangement and geometric parameters of fins 
significantly impact the heat transfer rate of PCM [ 142 ]. Previous 
research has employed single factor analysis methods [ 143 ] or response 
surface methodology [ 144 ] to optimize the heat transfer performance of 
PCM or ascertain the influence of geometric parameters. However, the 
aforementioned optimization methods are limited in their applicability 
to the analysis of a relatively small number of parameters and struggle to 
achieve global optimization results, particularly when dealing with 
complex structural fins in multi-parameter or multi-objective optimi -
zation problems. Nevertheless, advancements in computer technology 
and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) have made it possible to utilize 
intelligent optimization algorithms for achieving global optimization of 
fin structures integrated within LHS units.
Fig. 13 presents the optimization studies of geometric parameters of 
fins integrated into the LHS unit. Maleki et al. [ 145 ] and Xu et al. [ 146 ] 
implemented rectangular fins in rectangular and square PCM cavities. 
Maleki et al. considered the number, length, and volume fraction of fins 
as optimization variables, and performed multi-objective optimization 
with phase change time and heat storage as objective functions. The 
mathematical relationship between the optimization variables and the 
objective function was established based on the ANN model ’ s prediction 
of CFD simulation data. This study successfully achieved Pareto opti -
mality of fin parameters across various target weights. It was observed 
that around 60 % of the Pareto optimal non-dimensional fin lengths (l =

fins exhibited a volume fraction below 0.1. Furthermore, 81 % of the 
Pareto optimal fins were found to possess either 4 or 5 fins. In the 
research conducted by Xu et al. [ 146 ], the optimization variables 
encompassed the number, position, length, and thickness of fins, while 
the optimization objective function revolved around the dimensionless 
melting time. The integration of CFD and GA was employed for

in the absence of constraints on fin thickness, maximizing the length of

Biomimetic techniques have been extensively utilized across various 
domains of flow and heat transfer [ 150 , 151 ], encompassing the reduc -
tion of flow resistance, augmentation of heat transfer, and optimization 
of design. In the investigations conducted by Zheng [ 147 ] and Shen 
[ 148 ], Y-shaped and tree-shaped fins were employed, respectively, to 
amplify the heat transfer rate of PCMs in shell-and-tube LHS units. In 
these two studies, both the length and angle of the branches were 
regarded as optimization variables while ensuring a constant heat 
transfer area. Zheng et al. [ 147 ] employed the minimum distance be -
tween PCM and fins or cold sources as the objective function. They 
utilized a GA to optimize fins with arrow shapes. Results indicated that 
when the number of fins is 2, arrow shaped fins outperformed traditional 
Y-shaped fins by reducing the complete curing time by over 52.8 % and 
enhancing the heat dissipation rate by more than 110 %. Shen et al. 
[ 148 ] employed a computational fluid dynamics (CFD) coupled GA 
approach to optimize the geometric parameters of tree-shaped fins, 
taking into account the melting and solidification processes of PCM. The 
optimized tree-shaped fins exhibited a reduction in phase transition time 
by 38.83 % during the melting process, as well as a shortened solidifi -
cation process by 32.71 %, when compared to radial fins. Furthermore, 
the optimization results revealed that the optimized tree fins, designed 
for various heat transfer processes, displayed a gradual increase in 
branch length and a gradual decrease in branch angle. In a separate 
investigation [ 149 ], the researcher examined the collective impacts of 
incorporating fins on the augmentation of thermal storage rate and 
reduction of thermal storage capacity. The authors employed the energy 
stored per unit mass ( E m) and the average power ( P t) as multi-objective 
functions, and conducted optimization of the width and height of the 
upper and lower fins of the LHS unit utilizing NSGA-II. Optimization 
yielded a notable augmentation in power output while upholding a 
minimal compromise on the stored potential energy. In scenarios where 
power and stored energy held equal significance, the optimization 
outcome manifested as a 90.1 % amplification in power-to-mass ratio 
and a 4.3 % reduction in stored energy.
The afore-mentioned studies on fin optimization have primarily 
focused on specific geometric parameters, such as thickness, length, 
angle, and arrangement position, as design variables. However, these 
optimizations have been limited in terms of degrees of freedom and 
direction. In recent years, topology optimization in the field of me -
chanical design has been introduced into the optimization of fins in LHS 
units and has received widespread attention. Topology optimization 
offers a greater number of design degrees of freedom and a larger design 
space compared to traditional size and shape optimization methods 
[ 152 , 153 ]. Several studies have demonstrated that the utilization of 
topology optimized fins leads to a notable enhancement in both the heat 
transfer rate and the uniformity of temperature distribution within PCM 
[ 154 – 156 ]. The Method of Moving Asymptote (MMA) algorithm was 
commonly employed in the topology optimization of LHS fins. MMA is a 
nonlinear optimization technique that involves the continuous adjust -
ment of asymptotes while considering constraints, making it applicable 
for solving diverse engineering and scientific problems. Nevertheless, an 
excessive number of objective functions or constraints may impede the 
effectiveness of MMA. Hence, it is recommended that forthcoming 
studies explore the potential of employing sophisticated AI optimization 
algorithms to address the multi-objective and multi-constraint optimi -
zation challenges pertaining to fin structures in LHS systems.
4.1.4. Arrangement of porous material
In order to enhance the effective thermal conductivity of PCM, the 
incorporation of porous materials with high thermal conductivity has 
been identified as a viable approach [ 157 , 158 ]. Previous research has 
primarily focused on uniformly distributing porous materials within the 
PCM. However, subsequent investigations [ 159 – 161 ] revealed that the 
presence of natural convection can cause the melting front of PCM to 
deviate from parallel alignment with the heating boundary, leading to 
accelerated melting of the upper PCM (as depicted in Fig. 14 ). Moreover, 
the complete coverage of porous materials may impede the natural 
convection within the PCM. Hence, the utilization of non-uniformly 
arranged porous materials in the LHS unit can effectively augment the 
heat transfer rate of the phase change process [ 162 ]. Investigating the 
optimization of porous material parameters, including type, thickness, 
and porosity, to maximize the dynamic heat transfer performance of the 
LHS represents a significant research concern.

foam in each layer as a variable, constrained within the range of 50 % –

4.1.5. Cascaded latent heat storage system
To improve the heat transfer of PCMs at the system level, a recom -
mended approach is to employ multiple PCMs in the construction of a 
cascaded latent heat storage (CLHS) system [ 165 ]. Fig. 17 illustrates the 
schematic diagrams of the PCM configurations and the distribution of 
temperature differences in the CLHS system. Throughout the charging 
process, the temperature of the incoming HTF surpasses the phase 
transition temperature of all PCMs, with the phase transition tempera -
ture progressively decreasing along the direction of HTF flow. During 
the discharging process, the direction of flow for the HTF is opposite to 
that of the charging process. In the case of a single PCM system, the 
temperature difference for heat transfer between the HTF and PCM is 
notably diminished [ 166 ]. In contrast, the CLHS system is capable of 
sustaining a consistent temperature difference for heat transfer, thereby 
enhancing the overall heat transfer rate of the system. Based on statis -
tical data [ 167 ], the CLHS system exhibited notable improvements in 
various aspects when compared to a solitary PCM heat storage system.

The thermodynamic performance of the CLHS system is influenced 
by several crucial factors, namely the selection of materials, the distri -
bution of melting points, and the distribution of mass or volume of PCM 
at various stages. Previous studies [ 168–171 ] attempted to optimize the 
distribution of PCM melting points analytically by simplifying the heat 
transfer model and employing the Lagrange Multiplier Method. How -
ever, the reliance on numerous unreasonable assumptions in this 
approach may result in a lack of accuracy in the optimization results. 
Subsequently, progressive employment of AI-driven advanced optimi -
zation algorithms was observed in the pursuit of enhancing the perfor -
mance optimization of CLHS.
Table 4 shows the research on optimizing CLHS performance using 
intelligent algorithms. In line with the preceding sections, the optimi -
zation process primarily relied on intelligent algorithms such as GA and 
the Differential Evolution (DE) algorithm. The CLHS system encom -
passed various structures, including packed bed, shell-and-tube, plate, 
and others, as depicted in Fig. 18 . The majority of research endeavored 
concentrate on optimizing the volume distribution of PCMs [ 172–176 ]. 
This optimization was accomplished by regulating the height or length 
of each stage. Furthermore, several studies have examined the compo -
sition of PCM [ 172 ], mass distribution and the number of heat transfer 
units (NTUs) of each LHS unit [ 177 , 178 ]. In the study conducted by 
Zhang et al. [ 177 ], the researchers assumed that the latent heat and 
other physical parameters of PCM were identical, and they utilized the 
differential evolution (DE) algorithm to optimize the melting point of 
PCMs, mass, and NTU of each stage. Building upon this research, Shen 
et al. [ 178 ] further optimized the mass of PCMs and the NTU values of 
LHS units using a GA based on three confirmed commercial PCMs. This 
study successfully addressed the limitations of optimization error arising 
from unfounded assumptions in the research conducted by Zhang et al. 
[ 177 ].
The majority of optimization studies in this field primarily focus on 
maximizing the heat storage or release capacity as objective functions. 
However, studies [ 177 , 178 ] have also explored objective functions 
based on exergy and entransy. Both investigations indicated that the 
utilization of an objective function based on exergy was more appro -
priate for optimizing the performance of CLHS systems compared to 
objective functions based on energy and entransy. This is due to the fact 
that optimization outcomes based on the exergy objective function yield 
higher energy quality and require a smaller total PCM mass. The sole 
study [ 176 ] focused on optimizing the temperature rise rate of PCM, as 
CLHS was used for heat sink in a thermal management system. In gen -
eral, the intelligent optimization algorithms employed to optimize the 
CLHS system have resulted in notable enhancements in its heat storage 
capacity and temperature regulation.

only by the physical parameters of the PCM and the structural param -

particular, in complex systems comprising multiple functional compo -

influence on the overall performance compared to the structural pa -
rameters of the LHS unit. Hence, in contrast to the optimization of 
structural and layout aspects, the optimization of operational control 
strategies entails not only the consideration of the parameters pertaining 
to the left-hand side (LHS) unit itself, but also the operational parame -
ters of the entire system. For instance, as previously mentioned, N. 
Javani et al. [ 137 ] conducted a study where, in addition to optimizing 
the structure of the LHS unit, they employed GA to optimize the design 
of various operating parameters in another investigation [ 179 ]. This was 
done with the aim of attaining a multi-objective function that maximizes 
the system’s exergy efficiency while minimizing the total system cost

utilization of ML techniques in predicting properties of PCMs and

of the training database [ 185 ]. An augmentation in the magnitude of the 
training database has the potential to enhance the precision of pre -
dictions, albeit at the expense of a substantial rise in the cost associated 
with generating said training database. Insufficiency in the availability 
of data may result in diminished prediction accuracy or a deficiency in

TRNSYS or EnergyPlus. In contrast to the expenses associated with nu -
merical methods, the computational cost of ML prediction technology 
can be deemed insignificant. While numerous studies asserted that the 
anticipated outcomes exhibit a satisfactory margin of error when 
compared to numerical results, they fail to consider the errors stemming 
from the numerical research process itself.

5.2. AI optimization
According to the aforementioned analysis, it is evident that AI-based 
advanced optimization algorithms have been extensively employed in 
the optimization of PCM energy storage systems. These algorithms 
encompassed the optimization of both the structural layout of LHS units 
and the operational parameters of the system. Among the various opti -
mization techniques, the GA stoods out as the most frequently utilized 
method, while a minority of studies have also explored the application of 
particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm, differential evolution 
(DE) algorithm, and other similar approaches. Despite the findings of 
Cheng et al. ’ s study [ 139 ], which indicated that deep learning based 
DDQN optimization algorithms exhibit superior performance compared 
to NSGA-II and MOPSO algorithms, it is important to note that optimi -
zation algorithms utilizing ML or deep learning techniques are seldom 
employed in the context of PCM energy storage optimization. Conse -
quently, future research endeavors should acknowledge this gap and

Typically, optimization algorithms incorporate mathematical

flowchart that combines the widely employed mathematical model with 
GA and PSO algorithms. The thermodynamic parameters in the mathe -

with the dependent variables, which is solved by the mathematical

are often simplified, primarily focusing on energy conservation equa -

comprehensive resolution of the conservation equations encompassing 
mass, momentum, and energy has been successfully attained [ 136 , 163 ,

process, the majority of the optimization time is allocated to the itera -
tion of the CFD program rather than the GA iteration. Consequently, the 
most efficient strategy for minimizing the optimization time involves 
diminishing the reliance on CFD programs. Transient system energy 
consumption modeling software, such as TRNSYS, is utilized as a crucial 
tool in the optimization research of building energy systems, aiding in 
the resolution of simplified mathematical models [ 182 , 189 ].
One additional challenge encountered in the optimization of PCM 
energy storage using AI algorithms pertains to the generalizability of the 
optimization outcomes. It is not guaranteed that the results attained 
through optimization within specific boundaries and operating condi -
tions can be universally applied across all operating conditions. Current 
research predominantly concentrates on optimizing a solitary phase 
transition process, particularly in relation to the optimization of struc -
tural parameters of LHS components. The majority of optimizations are 
predicated upon the melting process of PCM, yet there remained a 
dearth of evaluation regarding the influence of optimal configuration on 
the comprehensive performance of the system ’ s charging-discharging 
cycle. Shen et al. [ 148 ] conducted a study which revealed that the 
optimal configuration attained through the optimization of charging and 
discharging processes diverges, despite exhibiting similar distribution 
characteristics. Furthermore, owing to the protracted duration of the 
PCM phase transition process, it is imperative to judiciously select an 
appropriate time point for numerical calculation and optimization. It is 
challenging to guarantee that the optimal configuration achieved 
through optimization at a specific time point during the phase transition 
process will yield optimal performance at other time points or 
throughout the entire phase transition cycle. In subsequent research, it is 
imperative to investigate the criteria for selecting appropriate time 
points for optimization and assess the influence of optimization out -
comes at different time points on the performance of other time points. 
When undertaking multi-objective optimization, the objectives that pose 
the greatest conflict are the maximization of thermodynamic perfor -
mance parameters and the minimization of investment and operating 
costs. The prevalent approach to address this is through the utilization of 
linear weighting and the principles of Pareto optimality as employed in 
MOGA and MOPSO. It is important to note that the outcomes of a linear 
weighted model are heavily reliant on the weight coefficients, thus 
necessitating their careful consideration during the optimization 
process.
In the context of hybrid energy systems, a multitude of design vari -
ables necessitate consideration, encompassing both geometric parame -
ters of components and system operating parameters. Prior to 
optimization, it is imperative to conduct sensitivity analysis to ascertain 
the pivotal factors influencing performance. Nevertheless, it is crucial to 
also account for practical considerations during application, whereby 
design variables that prove excessively challenging to modify are 
deemed unsuitable. When evaluating the objective function in terms of 
economic parameters, it is crucial to consider variations in energy pri -
ces, interest rates, and inflation rates across different regions. Previous 
studies have demonstrated [ 184 ] that optimization outcomes differ 
significantly depending on the region, primarily due to disparities in 
climate conditions, energy prices, and economic circumstances.

in hybrid smart energy systems, which, when combined with AI pre -
diction and optimization algorithms, present formidable challenges in 
terms of computational power and time expenditure. Furthermore, the 
concerns regarding the precision of the surrogate model and the 
dependability of the optimization outcomes are noteworthy, as an 
excessive number of iterations may result in the accumulation of fore -
cast and optimization inaccuracies. Considering both temporal expenses 
and dependability, as depicted in Fig. 26 , it is advisable to adopt a route

pure PCM poses challenges in meeting power supply requirements. 
Consequently, composite PCM materials are being explored as potential 
energy storage solutions for smart energy systems. The thermal prop -

specific heat capacity, latent heat, and effective thermal conductivity, all 
of which are notably influenced by both the PCM and the matrix ma -

emerge as significant factors.

global final energy consumption and total carbon dioxide emissions

neutrality is of utmost importance [ 191 , 192 ]. A significant challenge in

function lies in determining the carbon emission factors, which neces -

the various types of auxiliary energy. It is crucial to note that the carbon 
emission factor is not a fixed value but rather varies over time and in 
different environmental conditions, thus requiring regular updates and 
timely corrections [ 193 ].

assessment framework for thermal comfort.
6. Conclusions
This study provides a comprehensive review of the utilization of 
artificial intelligence (AI) technology in phase change material (PCM) 
energy storage. The review primarily focuses on its application in solar 
thermal utilization systems, electric vehicle/electronic device thermal 
management systems, and building energy efficiency systems. Based on 
the functional aspects of AI in PCM energy storage, the current research 
on monitoring can be categorized into prediction and optimization. The 
limitations of previous research have been summarized and potential 
avenues for improvement have been proposed. Furthermore, the pri -
mary directions and challenges of future investigations have been 
highlighted, accompanied by suggested strategies. The main findings 
can be summarized as follows. 
1) In the field of AI prediction and optimization research pertaining to 
PCM energy storage, the most prevalent methods are artificial neural 
networks (ANN), genetic algorithms (GA), and their respective de -
rivative models and algorithms.
2) The effectiveness of the surrogate model, which is generated using 
machine learning (ML) algorithms, is not consistently reliable and is 
significantly influenced by the dataset size. The literature review 
reveals a wide range of dataset sizes, ranging from 16 to 500,000. In 
the investigation of predicting the physical properties of PCM, the 
dataset primarily comprises experimental data. Conversely, in the 
examination of performance prediction for LHS systems, more than 
70 % of the dataset is derived from numerical studies. In future ac -
ademic investigations, it is recommended that emphasis be placed on 
the exploration of a well-balanced approach in PCM energy storage 
prediction modeling, which effectively addresses both the accuracy 
of predictions and the associated costs of training databases.
3) Prior to implementing a predictive model in a dissimilar setting to its 
training process, it is imperative to assess the model’s capacity for 
generalization, particularly in environments characterized by sig -
nificant variations in input variable types and boundary conditions. 
Regrettably, the investigation of most AI applications in PCM energy 
storage currently overlooks this crucial aspect. The utilization of 
Bayesian regularization and hybrid models can enhance both pre -
diction accuracy and generalization ability.
4) The predominant focus in the field of AI application in PCM energy 
storage lies in the optimization of the structural layout of LHS units, 
as well as the operational parameters and strategies of the system. 
The primary objective in optimizing the layout of LHS unit structures 
is to enhance the heat transfer rate of PCM, thereby making the 
parameter associated with the charging/discharging rate the usual 
objective function. On the other hand, the optimization of system 
operating parameters and strategies places greater emphasis on the 
overall tech-economic performance of the system.
5) Optimization algorithms incorporating ML or deep learning meth -
odologies are infrequently employed in the context of PCM energy 
storage optimization, warranting increased attention in forthcoming 
research endeavors. This is substantiated by existing studies, which 
indicate their superior efficacy compared to conventional GA and 
PSO optimization outcomes.
6) The combination of computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and GA 
represents an emerging optimization approach that enhances the 
precision of the model-solving process. However, this method comes 
at the expense of increased computational time. To minimize the 
duration of the optimization process, it is advisable to decrease 
reliance on CFD programs, as this has been found to be the most 
effective strategy.
7) There are differences in the optimal configuration obtained based on 
PCM melting and solidification process optimization. Nevertheless, 
most optimizations are based on the single process of PCM melting or 
solidification, and there is still a lack of evaluation of the compre -
hensive performance of optimized configurations in charge- 
discharge cycles.
8) In the context of multi-objective optimization for PCM energy stor -
age, the conflicting objective functions involve the maximization of 
thermodynamic performance parameters and the minimization of 
investment and operating costs. To address this, a commonly 
employed approach is the utilization of linear weighting and the 
Pareto optimality principle. However, it is important to note that the 
outcomes of linear weighted models heavily rely on the assigned 
weight coefficients, thus necessitating careful consideration during 
the optimization process.
9) A route of AI in hybrid smart energy systems has been proposed, 
indicating the direction for efficient and flexible energy supply in the 
future. This route additionally considers carbon emissions and indi -
vidual thermal comfort as optimization objectives, while also high -
lighting the particular challenges that this research entails.
