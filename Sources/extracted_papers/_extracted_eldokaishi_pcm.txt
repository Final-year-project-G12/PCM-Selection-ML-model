ABSTRACT  
Numerical modeling of solar thermal storage systems is often challenged with limitations on the computational 
effort due to their transient non-linear behavior that dictates accurate modeling of the physics over long-term 
operations (i.e., annual). Such challenges result into scarcity of literature on comprehensive design guidelines 
for solar thermal storage systems. This study presents a framework through which the potential of artificial 
neural network (ANN) modeling of a hybrid solar thermal storage system involving phase change materials is 
extensively investigated. An experimentally validated numerical model for the system is used to generate the 
training and testing datasets for the ANN model. The effect of changing the sampling method and the number of 
training samples is studied on the ANN model prediction. The results show that Sobol sequence sampling is 
superior to other sampling methods especially for low number of samples. The best sampling method is utilized 
to generate the training dataset with which the hyperparameters of the learning algorithm are optimized. The 
optimized ANN model is ultimately used to predict the system solar fraction under various design conditions to 
develop design maps that offer better visualization and sizing guidelines for the hybrid solar thermal storage 
systems. ANN is shown to offer a potential candidate for accurate and computationally efficient modeling of 
complex thermal systems. Upon proper configuration and training, the ANN model can accurately (i.e., a co -
efficient of determination of 0.9999) predict the performance of the hybrid thermal storage system with 
approximately five orders of magnitude reduction in computational time compared to conventional numerical 
models.   
1. Introduction 
Solar radiation is an abundant and clean source of renewable energy; 
however, its intermittent nature poses limitations on utilizing its full 
potential. This created significant research attraction toward thermal 
energy storage as a means to bridge the mismatch between the avail -
ability of solar energy and the thermal demand. Water tank storage is the 
most prominent in domestic heating applications due primarily to 
design simplicity and low cost. Besides being an abundant and cheap 
storage medium, water is privileged with superior thermal properties (i. 
e., heat capacity and thermal conductivity) compared to other storage 
mediums. The primary drawback manifested by sensible energy storage 
mediums, like water, when compared to latent energy storage is their 
lower storage densities, resulting in large operating temperature swings, 
and thus shorter discharge periods. This necessitates significantly larger 
tank volumes per unit stored energy. 
Phase Change Materials (PCMs) qualify as an energy storage medium 
that stores thermal energy in the form of latent heat. This paper focuses 
on solid – liquid PCMs that melt during the charging periods and solidify 
during the discharging periods. There is a large body of literature that 
focuses on the impact of integrating PCMs in solar thermal storage ap -
plications. Mazman et al. [1] experimentally investigated the effect of 
incorporating three different PCM materials at the top of a stratified 
solar domestic hot water (SDHW) storage tank. It was shown that 
paraffin wax and stearic acid provided significant enhancement in the 
thermal performance of the SDHW system. Cabeza et al. [2] incorpo -
rated cylindrical granular graphite PCM compound in a stratified SDHW 
tank while varying the number of PCM modules. Their results showed 
that increasing the number of PCM modules increased the storage den -
sity thus allowing the use of smaller tanks for the same purpose. Mehling 
et al. [3] showed that integrating cylindrical PCM modules at the top of a 
storage tank increased the storage density by 20 – 40%. Abdelsalam et al. 
[4] showed that introducing PCM modules in solar domestic hot water 
tanks resulted in around 40% reduction in tank volume compared to 
sensible storage. Although integrating PCM modules in the storage tanks

computationally efficient, yet accurate modeling of solar thermal stor -

2. System description

Abdelsalam et al. [4,17] for the water-PCM thermal storage system

and testing cases for the ANN model. Fig. 1 shows the layout of the 
hybrid storage system connected to a flat-plate solar collector loop and a 
demand-side loop. The hybrid storage tank is equipped with an

the top for discharging. The solar collector side is considered the 
charging loop and the demand side is considered the discharging loop.

4. Artificial neural network modeling 
Artificial neural networks are data processing systems capable of 
analyzing and correlating input and output data. The fundamental ele -
ments that make up the ANN are called neurons. These neurons are 
interconnected with synaptic weights. Each neuron applies certain 
mathematical functions to its inputs and then communicates the results 
with other neurons as shown in Fig. 4 . ANN has the capability of 
recognizing patterns of multi-dimensional complex datasets and 
providing a generalized correlation between the inputs and outputs that 
enables predicting the output of new data points. For more details about 
ANN modeling, refer to [20] .

5. Results and discussion 
This section is organized to show the effect of different sampling 
techniques, number of samples, and hyperparameters tuning on the 
predictive capabilities of the neural network. Then, the utility of the 
optimized ANN model is demonstrated through the generation of design 
maps for the solar hybrid storage system.  
a. Sampling method and number of samples 
For the ANN model to accurately simulate the solar thermal storage 
system, it must train on a set of data strategically selected to cover the 
studied range summarized in Table 1 . Each data point (training sample) 
contains different values for the five input parameters (collector area, 
storage tank volume, load temperature, PCM melting temperature, and 
PCM volume fraction) and the corresponding output (solar fraction). As 
such, there are two parameters that need to be carefully determined: 
which sampling method to be utilized to sample the studied range and 
what is the minimum number of training samples required to adequately 
train the ANN model. Three sampling techniques were investigated: 
Sobol sequence, Latin hypercube, and Monte Carlo. A python code is 
developed to sample the studied range. A training set up to 10,000 
training samples was generated utilizing each sampling method. ANN 
model was created and trained using each sampling method at the 
following number of training sample increments (250, 500, 1000, 1500, 
2000, 3000, 4000, 5000, 6000, 7000, 8000, 9000, 10000) totaling 13 
models for each sampling method (39 models overall). A comprehensive 
testing set of 84,480 samples covering the entire studied range was 
created (these testing samples were not introduced to the ANN models 
during the training phase and thus will test the predictive and general -
ization ability of each model). The testing MAE, RMSE, and the coeffi -
cient of determination ( R
2
) for the sampling methods are plotted against 
the number of samples, as shown in Figs. 5-7 , respectively. 
It is observed that the Sobol sequence sampling outperforms other 
sampling techniques, providing 44% and 16% reduction in the testing 
mean absolute error (MAE) compared to LHS models and MC models, 
respectively. This is found to agree with Burhenne et al. [26] whose 
results showed favorable performance of the Sobol-based sampling over 
other techniques. Increasing the number of training samples results in a 
more accurate ANN predictions. However, In the limit of larger number 
of samples, the different sampling techniques provide similar prediction

b. Hyperparameter optimization 
Hyperparameter optimization is the process of configuring the ANN 
hyperparameters. An ANN model with a properly configured hyper -
parameters will lead to an accurate ANN predictions (the ANN will 
mimic the studied system accurately). The optimization of the model 
hyperparameters is carried out for the Sobol sequence sampling ANN 
model with a total of 3,000 training samples. The optimizer is a machine 
learning algorithm used to adjust the synaptic weights during the 
training process with the goal of minimizing the loss function. Adaptive 
moment estimation learning algorithm, known as “ Adam ” algorithm 
[27] , is simple and computationally efficient for gradient-based opti -
mization of stochastic objective functions. It is well poised for machine 
learning problems with large datasets and/or multi-dimensional 
parameter spaces. As such, this study employs the Adam learning al -
gorithm to minimize the loss function. A methodical approach is 
developed to optimize the key hyperparameters of the learning algo -
rithm: the learning rate, the number of hidden layers, the number of 
neurons per hidden layer, and the activation function, to enhance the 
predictive capability of the ANN model.  
i. Learning rate 
The learning rate (LR) in machine learning is the step size after each 
epoch towards minimizing the loss function. Higher learning rates often 
results in faster training on the expense of model training stability. 
Conversely, smaller learning rates yields slower yet more stable training 
process, but it might cause the loss function to get stuck in an undesir -
able local minimum [28] . Therefore, an optimum learning rate is 
required to reach the global minimum value of the loss function while 
preserving the model training stability. Table 2 shows the LR optimi -
zation process. A learning rate of 0.005 is seen to achieve the lowest 
testing error.  
ii. ANN topology 
ANN consists of layers of interconnected neurons. A typical ANN 
consists of an input layer, one or multiple hidden layers, and an output 
layer. Varying the number of hidden layers and the neurons in each 
hidden layer has a great effect on the performance of ANN models [29] . 
Lower number of hidden layers simplifies the model on the expense of its 
ability to effectively capture the complexities and non-linearities in the 
modelled system. On the other hand, increasing the number of hidden 
layers beyond a certain value can lead to model overfitting. Table 3 
shows the effect of varying the number of hidden layers from 1 to 4 and 
the number of neurons in each hidden layer to 30, 40, and 50. It is 
observed that 3 hidden layers with 50 neurons in each hidden layer 
provide the optimum structure to achieve improved predictions.  
iii. Activation function 
Activation functions also known as “ transfer functions ” are used to 
limit the neuron output within a reasonable range of values [30] . 
Without activation functions the ANN will simply be a linear regression 
model. Activation functions are applied after the mathematical pro -
cesses in the neuron. There is a variety of activation functions available 
in the literature, each having its advantages and limitations. For 
example, rectified linear activation function (RELU) is computationally 
efficient but not suitable for inputs that approach zero or negative 
values, whereas Softmax activation function is suitable for classification 
problems. Table 4 shows a comparison of the ANN model performance 
under various activation functions. It is observed that sigmoid activation 
function is the most suitable for this study.  
iv. Single vs. Multi-ANN models for prediction 
To further improve the predictive capabilities of the ANN model, the 
best three models during the training and the testing stages are selected 
for prediction. The outlier prediction out of the three is eliminated for

6. Conclusions 
The potential of ANN modeling is extensively investigated for a 
hybrid solar thermal storage system involving PCMs for domestic water 
heating applications. The effects of using different sampling methods, 
number of samples, and hyperparameters tuning are studied. A total of 
39 ANN models are developed to investigate the effect of sampling 
methods and number of samples on the predictive capabilities of the 
ANN models. The results show that the Sobol sequence sampling pro -
vides less overall error in the testing sets compared to the Latin hyper -
cube and the Monte Carlo sampling methods, especially for low number 
of data points in the training set. Hyperparameter optimization shows 
the effect of tuning the ANN hyperparameters on the predictive capa -
bilities of the ANN models. The results show that ANN can be used 
successfully to model the hybrid solar thermal storage systems with an 
R
2 
value of 0.9999. The optimized ANN model is then deployed to 
generate design maps for the system, offering accessible sizing and se -
lection guidelines for the key design parameters: the storage tank vol -
ume, the PCM volume fraction and the PCM melting temperature. PCM 
parameter visualization shows that proper selection of the tank volume 
and the PCM melting temperature and volume fraction is essential to 
improve the system solar fraction. One of the main benefits of using ANN 
modeling is the significant reduction in the computational effort. The 
time spent to simulate the entire testing set (84,480 testing samples) 
using the numerical model by Abdelsalam et al. [4] is recorded to be 
over 120 h compared to around 5 s using the ANN model. As such, with 
careful configuration and training, ANN modeling can provide compu -
tationally efficient means to predict the performance of highly transient 
and non-linear systems. The framework presented in this article can be 
extended to involve other design and operational parameters such as the 
solar collector area, the weather profile, the demand profile to develop 
more comprehensive design maps for solar thermal storage systems.
