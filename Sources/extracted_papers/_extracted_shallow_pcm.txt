ABSTRACT
This study presents the experimental validation and enhanced thermal modeling of a Shallow Solar Pond (SSP) 
operating under three distinct heat extraction modes: direct, open cycle, and closed cycle. A custom-designed 
SSP, insulated and equipped with double-glazing and a PVC heat exchanger and black-painted bottom, was 
monitored under clear-sky conditions. 14 Artificial Neural Network (ANN) configurations were trained, with the 
optimal Bayesian Regularization-based model (two hidden layers, 15 neurons) achieving exceptional predictive 
accuracy (R
2 
= 0.99919, RMSE = 32.7580 %, MRE = 0.62 %) for temperatures across all system components and 
operational modes. The ANN model effectively captured complex nonlinear dynamics, including transient 
heating and cooling phases. The integration of this ANN with a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework 
enabled real-time optimization of the fluid flow rate in the closed-cycle mode, which was identified as the most 
efficient configuration. The MPC-ANN strategy reduced the outlet temperature tracking error by over 50 % 
(MAE = 1.42 
◦
C vs. 2.97 
◦
C without MPC), demonstrating superior thermal stability and adaptive control under 
fluctuating solar irradiance. This work confirms the closed-cycle SSP ’ s superiority for stable energy delivery and 
establishes a robust ANN-MPC framework for real-time operational optimization of solar thermal systems.
1. Introduction
The development of renewable energy technologies is a global pri -
ority due to the growing demand for sustainable energy solutions. One of 
the most promising alternatives is solar energy for its abundance and 
environmentally friendly nature [ 1 ]. Among various solar energy tech -
nologies, the shallow solar pond (SSP) has attracted significant attention 
due to its simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and the ability to store thermal 
energy and use it for various applications [ 2 ]. An SSP typically consists 
of a shallow water body with a blackened bottom designed to absorb and 
retain solar radiation, converting it into heat [ 3 ]. This stored heat can be 
harnessed for tasks such as water heating, industrial processes, and even 
power generation. Several design enhancements, including the use of 
transparent covers, thermal insulation, and integrated heat exchangers, 
have been investigated to boost system efficiency [ 4 ]. Heat extraction 
from SSPs is categorized into three primary methods: the direct method, 
which involves draining the pond to access heated water, albeit with 
lower efficiency; the open-cycle mode, where a heat exchanger circu -
lates fluid through the pond; and the closed-cycle mode, which employs 
a sealed heat exchanger system to reduce thermal losses and optimize 
heat transfer [ 4 – 6 ].
Significant research advancements have refined SSP technology. 
Early work by Dickinson et al. [ 3 ] demonstrated the feasibility of 
large-scale power generation using SSPs, emphasizing transparent 
plastic layers to minimize heat loss. Further studies [ 4 , 5 ] underscored 
the importance of optimizing pond depth, glazing materials, and oper -
ational modes, achieving efficiencies of up to 50 %. More recently, the 
introduction of nanofluids, such as aluminum oxide (Al

oxide (CuO), has been explored to enhance heat transfer efficiency [ 7 ]. 
To further improve thermal storage capabilities and reduce energy los -

efficiencies up to 60 % under specific conditions [ 6 ]. Experimental work 
by Ali et al. [ 8 ] validated the influence of water depth and glazing 
configurations on performance, while [ 9 ] confirmed the effectiveness of

As the design of SSP is also progressing, Artificial Neural Networks 
(ANNs) are turning out to be indispensable in the simulation, optimi -
zation, and modeling of renewable energy systems. ANNs are artificial 
intelligence algorithms that emulate the working of the human brain to 
learn from data and recognize patterns in data sets, using interconnected 
nodes. Because of their flexibility, they are very useful in solving 
nonlinear problems in energy studies [ 13 ]. Various types of ANNs, 
including multilayer perceptron (MLP) and radial basis function (RBF) 
networks, have been widely used in solar energy applications. MLP 
models, known for their simple architecture and efficient training, have 
been applied to forecast solar radiation, optimize energy efficiency, and 
predict thermal performance [ 14 ]. ANN models have demonstrated high 
accuracy in estimating heat transfer rates for parabolic trough, evacu -
ated tube, and solar air collectors [ 14 – 16 ]. Comprehensive reviews 
further confirm the effectiveness of ANN in predicting thermal perfor -
mance across various solar thermal systems [ 17 ]. In addition, 
ANN-based approaches have been applied to evaluate the impact of 
nanofluids, such as Cu

, on solar system efficiency, providing 
insights into their potential to enhance thermal performance [ 18 , 19 ].
The combination of ANN models with renewable energy research has 
contributed to improved design and operational strategies for solar 
thermal systems, including photovoltaic/thermal configurations, solar 
dryers, and solar-assisted heat pumps [ 20 ].
The increasing use of ANNs in renewable energy systems highlights 
their importance in enhancing sustainable energy performance. In this 
context, the present study aims to develop, validate, and optimize an 
ANN-based model for predicting the thermal behavior of an SSP oper -
ating under three different heat extraction modes: direct, open, and 
closed cycles. Experimental data were collected from a custom-built 1 
m
2 
SSP operating under identical environmental conditions for 
comparative analysis. The developed ANN model was trained not only to 
predict thermal performance with high accuracy but was also seamlessly 
integrated into a real-time Model Predictive Control (MPC) system. This 
integration enabled intelligent, adaptive regulation of key operating 
parameters most notably, the fluid flow rate to dynamically optimize 
heat extraction efficiency under varying solar conditions.
The novelty of this research is threefold. First, it presents a rigorous 
comparative experimental analysis of three distinct heat extraction 
modes direct, open cycle, and closed cycle conducted under identical 
environmental conditions, a comprehensive approach seldom achieved 
in existing literature. Second, it advances beyond conventional predic -
tive modeling by embedding the ANN as a core component within a real- 
time control framework, facilitating online thermal optimization. Third, 
the SSP was specially designed, constructed, and instrumented to enable 
high-resolution, real-time thermal monitoring across multiple points, 
significantly enhancing the reliability and fidelity of both model training 
and experimental validation.

Section 8 summarizes the key findings and contributions.
2. Construction of the system and data collection
Fig. 1 illustrates the experimental setup of the SSP. The system was 
constructed using galvanized sheet materials with dimensions of 0.76 ×
1.30 m and a depth of 0.06 m, accommodating a water capacity of 
approximately 60 L. To minimize conductive heat losses, the pond was 
thermally insulated with a 0.04 m polystyrene layer, while the bottom 
surface was painted black to maximize solar radiation absorption. Heat 
extraction was achieved through a transparent PVC heat exchanger, 10 
m in length and 0.008 m in diameter, connected to a QR30E brushless 
pump and a 20-L insulated storage tank. Although the effectiveness ( ε ) 
of the heat exchanger was not directly measured in this work, its thermal 
performance can be reasonably inferred from the material properties. 
Considering the thermal conductivity of PVC ( λ ≈ 0.19 W/m ⋅ K) and the 
tube wall thickness ( δ = 0.001 m), the overall heat transfer coefficient 
(U) is expected to be relatively low compared to metallic exchangers. 
However, the experimental results showed a stable temperature rise and 
consistent heat transfer, indicating that the exchanger operated with 
satisfactory effectiveness. Tap water was employed as the working fluid 
due to its availability, cost-effectiveness, and favorable thermal prop -
erties. Its main thermophysical characteristics are summarized in 
Table 1 , including thermal conductivity (k = 0.6 W/m ⋅ K), specific heat 
capacity (Cp = 4180 J/kg ⋅ K), and density ( ρ = 1000 kg/m
3
). These 
properties justify its selection as a working fluid, as they ensure efficient 
heat transfer and reliable operation at low cost compared to alternative 
engineered fluids. The SSP was covered with two glass panels, each 
0.003 m thick, separated by a 0.03 m air gap. The use of double glazing 
serves multiple purposes: (i) reducing convective heat losses through the 
insulating air gap, (ii) enhancing the greenhouse effect by transmitting 
short-wave solar radiation while restricting long-wave thermal emis -
sion, and (iii) protecting the pond against condensation, dust, and 
environmental effects. As noted by Kamiuto et al. [ 21 ], the application 
of multilayer transparent insulation significantly improves the thermal 
performance of solar pond systems by mitigating upward heat losses.
It should be noted that the three experiments were conducted on 
different consecutive days (August 15 – 17), under clear-sky conditions 
without significant weather fluctuations. This ensured that variations in 
solar radiation or ambient temperature did not bias the comparison 
across modes. Details of the main materials and components used in 
constructing the SSP are provided in Table 1 , which complements the 
schematic illustrated in Fig. 1 .
2.1. Experimental scenarios
To investigate the performance of the SSP under different thermal 
extraction strategies, three experimental scenarios were designed. The 
rationale for selecting direct, open, and closed cycles lies in their distinct 
heat transfer characteristics and their relevance to practical solar ther -
mal applications. The direct mode represents the simplest case, the open 
cycle reflects intermediate efficiency with continuous heat removal, 
while the closed cycle provides maximum isolation and storage capacity. 
These scenarios were therefore chosen to cover the full spectrum of SSP 
operation strategies reported in the literature.

The experiments were not operated simultaneously, but separately, 
one per day. This approach was necessary to avoid thermal interference 
between modes and to ensure independent performance evaluation of 
each configuration: 
1. Direct mode heat extraction (August 15)
In the first scenario, thermal energy was extracted using the direct 
mode, the simplest approach among the three (see Fig. 2 ). Heated water 
was drained directly from the pond, allowing immediate use of the 
stored heat. While this method enabled quick access to thermal energy, 
it resulted in significant heat losses due to exposure and the lack of 
thermal insulation or recovery mechanisms. 
2. Open cycle heat extraction (August 16)
The second scenario implemented an open-cycle continuous flow 
system. In this configuration, a heat exchanger placed inside the pond 
(illustrated in Fig. 3 ) transferred heat to a circulating working fluid, 
which continuously removed thermal energy from the pond. This setup 
enabled steady and sustained heat transfer, making it more suitable for 
applications that require a continuous thermal supply. However, being 
an open cycle, some thermal losses still occurred. 
3. Closed cycle heat extraction with thermal storage tank (August 17)
In the third scenario, the system operated in a closed-cycle contin -
uous flow heating mode, where the working fluid circulated in a sealed 
loop between the SSP and an insulated storage tank. A serpentine heat 
exchanger was placed inside the pond to collect thermal energy, and a 
second heat exchanger was installed inside the thermal storage tank to 
transfer the collected heat as shown in Fig. 4 . This design effectively 
isolated the working fluid from direct contact with the pond water, 
significantly reducing heat losses. As a result, it offered higher thermal 
efficiency and longer heat retention, making it more suitable for appli -
cations requiring stable and delayed thermal delivery. This design 
effectively minimized contact with the environment, thus reducing heat 
losses and enhancing long-term heat storage, making it suitable for 
delayed thermal utilization.
2.2. Data collection and analysis
Data acquisition was performed using an Arduino UNO microcon -
troller, complemented by various sensors, with measurements recorded 
every minute from 07:00 to 19:00 h. This experimental procedure was 
strictly followed for each of the three scenarios to maintain consistency 
and comparability across modes. The ambient air temperature and hu -
midity were monitored using a DHT22 sensor, while seven DS18B20 
waterproof temperature probes were installed at critical locations, 
including the upper and lower glass covers, the storage tank, the 
absorber plate, the pond water, and the inlet/outlet of the heat 
exchanger. The QR30E brushless pump was employed to circulate the 
heat transfer fluid and overcome the pressure drop. Solar radiation was 
estimated using the semi-empirical Bird & Hulstrom model [ 22 ], based 
on the geographical coordinates of Tablat, Medea, Algeria.

3. Overview of ANNs
ANNs are an essential part of AI along with expert systems, fuzzy 
logic, and automatic control systems. ANNs provide a scientific 
approach to developing models that learn from data, identify patterns, 
and make data-driven decisions. The bio-inspired architecture of ANNs 
mimics the parallel processing capability of the human brain by utilizing 
interconnected processing units known as neurons [ 13 , 14 ].
3.1. Key features of ANNs
The effectiveness of ANNs lies in their ability to handle complex and 
imprecise data. The most significant features of ANNs include: 
• Pattern Recognition and Learning: ANNs can extract useful infor -
mation from noisy or complex data sets, making pattern recognition 
and classification of data possible.
• Adaptive Learning: ANNs adjust their internal parameters (weights) 
by training or experiences to enhance performance, learning to carry 
out data-dependent tasks.

• Self-Organization: ANNs self-organize their internal representation 
of data during learning, offering dynamic modeling of data.
• Parallel Processing: ANNs distributed structure supports simulta -
neous computations, enhancing processing efficiency and robustness 
[ 23 ].
3.2. ANN architecture and components
The architecture of ANNs consists of layers and neurons that are 
interconnected and each contributes to the overall functionality of the 
network. The most important components are:
3.2.1. Network layers
• Input Layer: Receives input features such as temperature, humidity, 
and solar radiation.
• Hidden Layers: Consist of artificial neurons that process input data 
using mathematical transformations, allowing the network to learn 
complex, non-linear relationships [ 18 ].
• Output Layer: Produces the ultimate output from calculations of the 
earlier layers, which is the network classification or prediction.
3.2.2. Artificial neurons and activation functions
Each neuron processes input data using weighted connections, a 
summation function, and an activation function. Common activation 
functions include: 
• Sigmoid Function: Smooth gradients are generated, which is appro -
priate for probabilistic outputs.
• Linear Function: Employed where regression is needed.
• Sign Function: Generates binary outputs, useful in classification.
• Step Function: Triggers neurons only when inputs surpass a certain 
threshold [ 23 ].
3.2.3. ANNs types
ANNs may be grouped based on their layer structure: 
• Single-layer Neural Networks: Consist of one processing layer, which 
feeds inputs straight into the output layer without any hidden layers, 
suitable for simple problems.
• Multi-layer Neural Networks: Are made up of more than one hidden 
layer, which raises the capacity of the network to model complicated 
relationships at the cost of greater computational complexity.

4. ANN training and optimization
To ensure the accurate thermal performance prediction of the SSP, 
different ANN training scenarios were considered. Training was focused 
on the optimization of the network performance by changing key 
hyperparameters, which consist of the training algorithm, the number of 
hidden layers, and the number of neurons in each layer.
4.1. Training algorithm selection
Various backpropagation-based training functions were attempted, 
including Bayesian Regularization (trainbr), Levenberg-Marquardt 
(trainlm), BFGS Quasi-Newton (trainbfg), and Scaled Conjugate 
Gradient (trainscg). All the algorithms were benchmarked against each 
other based on their error minimization and computational efficiency.
4.2. Data preprocessing and outlier treatment
Prior to training the ANN model, a data preprocessing phase was 
conducted to ensure the reliability of the dataset. Input variables 
including temperature, humidity, and solar radiation were normalized 
to enhance training convergence and reduce computational bias. To 
identify potential outliers, a combination of visual inspection and sta -
tistical criteria specifically the Z-score method — was applied. Data 
points deviating more than ± 3 standard deviations from the mean were 
considered potential outliers. However, analysis of the experimental 
data revealed no significant outliers that warranted exclusion. There -
fore, the entire dataset, consisting of approximately 2160 data points 
collected over three consecutive days (with a 1-min sampling interval 
from 07:00 to 19:00), was retained for model training, validation, and 
testing. The dataset was randomly divided into 70 % for training, 15 % 
for validation, and 15 % for testing to ensure the generalization capa -
bility of the ANN model (see Fig. 4 ).
The input and output variables used in the ANN model, as illustrated 
in Fig. 5 , were carefully selected to reflect the thermal dynamics of the 
SSP under all three heat extraction scenarios: direct mode, open-cycle

7. Model predictive control design and parameter identification
Building on the experimental comparison of the three extraction 
modes ( Fig. 13 ), it was shown that the closed cycle delivers the most 
stable and extended heat retention, ensuring minimal thermal losses and 
sustained elevated temperatures. This superior stability makes the 
closed configuration the most suitable candidate for advanced control 
enhancement. Accordingly, this section develops a hybrid ANN – MPC 
strategy aimed at regulating the fluid flow rate in the SSP closed cycle. 
The controller is designed with two main objectives: (i) minimizing the 
outlet temperature tracking error, and (ii) reducing the energy con -
sumption of the circulation pump. Implementation is carried out on the 
QR30E brushless DC pump, which operates within a constrained flow 
range, making it particularly well-suited for predictive optimization 
guided by the ANN model.
7.1. Controller concept
The MPC controller estimates the optimal flow rate by simulating the 
future outlet temperature response for a set of candidate flow rates. For 
each candidate value, the temperature correction is evaluated using a 
simplified dynamic model:

7.2. Neural Network-Based MPC control system architecture
Fig. 16 illustrates the architecture of an MPC system integrated with 
an ANN for effective temperature regulation. This hybrid system com -
bines the predictive capabilities of an ANN with the optimization po -
tential of MPC to ensure precise control over the system ’ s temperature.
7.2.1. System overview
The architecture consists of the following key components: 
• Input: The initial temperature and process input to the system.
• Function Fitting Neural Network (FNN): A data-driven model trained 
to predict the system ’ s output temperature T
fo-corr 
based on input 
dynamics and historical data.
• MPC Controller: Utilizes the predictions from the ANN to optimize 
the flow rate in order to minimize the error between the T
fo-pred 
and 
the T
fo-set
.
• Error Signal: The difference between the T
fo-pred 
and the T
fo-set 
serves 
as the primary input to the MPC, driving corrective actions to adjust 
the flow rate and maintain temperature stability.
7.2.2. System workflow
The system operates in a feedback loop, and its workflow can be 
broken down into the following steps: 
1. Neural Network Prediction:
The FNN processes the input data and simulates the expected output 
temperature T
fo-corr. 
The ANN ’ s predictive model captures the system ’ s 
dynamics, providing an estimation of the future temperature response. 
2. MPC Optimization:
The MPC controller evaluates the predicted temperature against the 
desired setpoint.
Using the predefined parameters α and β (discussed in Section 7.1 ), 
the MPC calculates the optimal mass flow rate. This optimization min -
imizes the error between the predicted temperature and the setpoint, 
ensuring efficient system operation. 
3. Closed-Loop Correction:
Based on the optimization by the MPC, the adjusted flow rate is 
applied to the system. The process is repeated in real-time, continuously 
correcting the flow rate to ensure temperature stability and to meet the 
target setpoint.

8. Conclusion
This study successfully demonstrated the superior thermal perfor -
mance of a closed-cycle SSP, insulated and equipped with double- 
glazing, a PVC heat exchanger, and a black-painted bottom, and the 
efficacy of a hybrid ANN and MPC framework for its optimization. 
Experimental results under identical environmental conditions 
confirmed that the closed-cycle mode outperformed both direct and 
open-cycle modes, providing higher peak temperatures, enhanced 
thermal stability, and prolonged heat retention due to minimized 
convective and evaporative losses.
The developed ANN model proved to be a powerful tool for pre -
dicting the complex, nonlinear thermal behavior of the SSP. The opti -
mally configured network (trainbr algorithm, 2 hidden layers, 15 
neurons) achieved remarkable accuracy across all measured tempera -
tures (T
C1
, T
C2
, T
wp
, T
p
, T
fo
, T
wt
) and under all operational modes, 
validating its capability to handle both steady-state and dynamic 
conditions.
The most significant contribution of this work is the integration of 
this accurate ANN model into a real-time MPC strategy. By dynamically 
adjusting the mass flow rate based on predictive control, the MPC – ANN 
system reduced thermal tracking errors by more than 50 %, significantly 
enhancing the system ’ s efficiency and robustness against solar irradi -
ance variations. The optimized control parameters ( α =   22.94, β =
33.23) for the closed-cycle operation highlight the potential for adap -
tive, energy-efficient management of solar thermal systems.
In summary, this research provides: 
1. A definitive experimental comparison establishing the closed-cycle 
configuration as the most efficient method for heat extraction from 
an SSP.
2. A highly accurate, data-driven ANN model for the thermal simula -
tion of SSPs.
3. A practical and intelligent MPC – ANN control framework that 
significantly improves real-time operational efficiency and thermal 
output stability.
This integrated approach paves the way for the intelligent automa -
tion and optimization of renewable energy systems, promising greater 
reliability and efficiency for practical, large-scale solar thermal appli -
cations. Future work will focus on extending this strategy to multi- 
objective optimization and real-world hardware-in-the-loop validation.
