# Literature Review for "Climate-Adaptive Intelligent Control and Optimization of PCM Thermal Storage for Solar Water Heating"

## TL;DR
- **Objective 1 is publishable but needs three upgrades to be defensible:** (i) add at least one *supervised* or *hybrid ML* layer so the "AI-enabled" label survives reviewer scrutiny (pure GMM + MCDM is increasingly classified as "MCDM," not "intelligent selection," in 2022–2026 material-selection literature); (ii) close the six self-identified data/method gaps with the standard references below (Kusuda–Achenbach for T_mains, clear-sky-constrained quantile mapping for GHI persistence, Kenisarin & Mahkamov for salt hydrates, Saaty's CR ≤ 0.10 for AHP, enthalpy-method supercooling/hysteresis in the validator); and (iii) reframe the physics-validation disagreement (negative Spearman ρ) as a *finding* — the MCDM ranking is dominated by supercooling, which the validator does not model, so the two cannot agree until supercooling is added to the tank model.
- **The full pipeline architecture (unsupervised climate clustering → PCM ranking → physics-based simulation validation) appears genuinely novel for solar water heating** — no single published study combines all three; the closest works each combine only two, which grounds a legitimate novelty claim.
- **For Objective 2 (DRL control), the literature strongly supports a discrete or hybrid action space (charge/discharge/bypass) with SAC or Dueling-DQN/PPO,** an EnergyPlus/TRNSYS-pretrained environment, and a multi-term reward balancing hot-water service (hard constraint), solar utilization, and auxiliary energy; this project's grey-box tank is on the low-fidelity end but is defensible if benchmarked against TRNSYS Type 840/860.

## Key Findings
1. **Pure GMM + MCDM sits at the weak end of what 2022–2026 literature calls "AI-enabled intelligent selection."** Recent PCM/material-selection papers increasingly pair MCDM with supervised ML, clustering, or optimization. To defend the "intelligent" label, the project should add a supervised classifier/regressor (Random Forest/XGBoost) or a hybrid ML+MCDM stage.
2. **The physics-validation disagreement is explainable and should be presented as a methodological result, not a failure.** Supercooling dominates the entropy weights in all three clusters but is absent from the grey-box validator, so the Spearman test literally cannot arbitrate the ranking.
3. **Every one of the six self-identified gaps maps to an established standard method** with a citable source (Part C).
4. **The end-to-end pipeline is novel;** the strongest benchmark competitors each combine only two of the three components.
5. **For DRL, SAC is the most-cited "best-balance" algorithm** for continuous thermal control, while DQN/Dueling-DQN suit discrete charge/discharge/bypass; reward shaping with a hard hot-water-service term is standard.

## Details

### PART A — What a strong "Objective 1" needs

**(A1) PCM selection for solar thermal / solar water heating via MCDM.**
- **Yadav et al. (2020/2023),** *Australian Journal of Mechanical Engineering* 21(1) — "Selection of phase change material for domestic water heating using multi-criteria decision approach." Uses AHP + entropy weights feeding TOPSIS, VIKOR, EXPROM2; ranks sodium acetate trihydrate + graphite, stearic acid, n-hexacosane. *Demonstrates:* the multi-method MCDM + dual (subjective + objective) weighting the project already uses — a direct benchmark. It includes salt hydrates, which the project's 55-row database lacks.
- A *Journal of Energy Storage* (2025) solar-desalination energy-storage-material study applies six MCDM methods (TOPSIS, EDAS, ARAS, VIKOR, MOORA) with entropy weighting to 14 PCM alternatives over 10 techno-environmental criteria. *Demonstrates:* incorporating environmental and cost criteria with real values — the project's cost/corrosion placeholders are a gap here.
- A Springer *Technical Physics* (2026) paper introduces a "comprehensive performance index" aggregating SAW, TOPSIS, GRA, VIKOR, PROMETHEE across 9 PCMs for solar DHW. *Demonstrates:* MCDM aggregation analogous to the project's Borda/Copeland step.

**(A2) AI/ML for PCM / thermal-material selection.**
- Random-forest / gradient-boosting / CatBoost regression to predict PCM-integrated envelope thermal performance from thermophysical inputs (~2021, 500 simulated data points). *Demonstrates:* supervised regression mapping PCM properties → performance — exactly the supervised layer the project lacks.
- Nature *Communications Engineering* (2026) "Predictive correlation of optimum phase change material for thermal energy storage." *Demonstrates:* a predictive correlation for optimum PCM selection.
- *Journal of Energy Storage* (2024) "The contribution of artificial intelligence to phase change materials in thermal energy storage: from prediction to optimization" — review documenting KNN, LASSO, XGBoost, SVM, Bayesian ridge for TES. *Demonstrates:* the accepted AI-in-PCM taxonomy; a good framing citation. (Well-established review, not a single result.)
- Bayesian optimization: *Computational Materials Science* (2022) used Gaussian-process Bayesian optimization over PCM physical properties for low-energy memory; a *Journal of Energy Storage* (2022) study used GP Bayesian optimization for effective thermal conductivity (RMSE < 2 K, R² 0.975–0.992, COMSOL-validated). *Demonstrates:* Bayesian optimization for PCM property targeting — an option for future work.
- Genetic algorithm / NSGA-II + MCDM: *Renewable Energy* (2019) multi-objective optimization of PCM TES for solar air systems (GA Pareto front + MCDM compromise selection); *Journal of Energy Storage* (2019) NSGA-II + LINMAP/TOPSIS for a PCM swimming-pool heater. *Demonstrates:* the GA/MOO+MCDM hybrid — a "gold-standard" benchmark.

**(A3) Climate-adaptive / climate-zone energy design (incl. India).**
- **Martínez-Gómez (2025),** *Energies* 18(22):5982 (published 14 Nov 2025) — "Comparative Framework for Climate-Responsive Selection of Phase Change Materials." AHP weights (melting point 47.5%, latent heat 25.7%, volumetric latent heat 13.5%, thermal conductivity 6.8%, specific heat 3.3%, density 3.3%) across COPRAS/VIKOR/TOPSIS/MOORA/PROMETHEE II for 16 PCMs in three predefined climate zones. It "consistently identified n-Heptadecane (C17) as the most suitable PCM for temperate and subtropical climates, while n-Octadecane (C18) and hydrated salts such as CaCl₂·6H₂O and Na₂CO₃·10H₂O were optimal for tropical zones" (temperate 18 °C, subtropical 23 °C, tropical 28 °C). *Demonstrates:* the single closest published analog to the project's climate-responsive MCDM — but zones are predefined (not clustered) and there is no dynamic simulation validation.
- **"A criteria-based climate classification approach considering clustering and building thermal performance: Case of India"** (2024), *Building and Environment* (article S0360132324013532) — k-means clustering of daily climatic variation, silhouette-validated, to update Indian climate zoning. *Demonstrates:* the exact clustering + building-performance-validation logic the project uses, applied to India — the strongest India-specific benchmark for the clustering half. A 2025 follow-up (article S0360132325011047) extends this to a district-level map.
- *Journal of Energy Storage* (2021) non-dimensional PCM performance parameters (PPP, PDR) for five Indian cities; and a suitability study identifying Eicosane and OM35 for Delhi (34–38 °C). *Demonstrates:* Indian-climate PCM mapping precedent.
- Indian climate-zone standards: **National Building Code of India 2016 (BIS)** and **ECBC 2017 (BEE)** define the five zones (hot-dry, warm-humid, composite, temperate, cold); Bansal & Minke (1988) is the historical origin. *Demonstrates:* the external-validation target for benchmarking the GMM regimes (like the Köppen ARI/NMI check).

**(A4) PCM property databases & sourcing (incl. salt hydrates/eutectics).**
- **Kenisarin & Mahkamov (2016),** *Solar Energy Materials and Solar Cells* 145:255–286 — salt hydrates thermophysical properties and costs (18 salt hydrates). *Demonstrates:* the authoritative salt-hydrate database to fill the project's missing inorganic candidates.
- **Sharma, Tyagi, Chen & Buddhi (2009),** *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews* 13(2):318–345 — the most-cited PCM review with property tables for organic/inorganic/eutectic PCMs. Also **Zalba et al. (2003),** *Applied Thermal Engineering* 23(3):251–283; **Cabeza et al. (2011),** *RSER* 15(3):1675–1695; **Kenisarin (2014),** *Solar Energy* 107:553–575 (organics); **Abhat (1983),** *Solar Energy* 30(4):313–332. *Demonstrates:* the canonical sourcing chain for PCM candidate data (all highly cited, foundational).
- Measurement/validation: DSC and T-history methods are standard for enthalpy, specific heat, and supercooling; reviews note salt-hydrate property data are scattered and variable, motivating cross-source validation (e.g., sodium acetate trihydrate can supercool ~71 °C below its melting point). *Demonstrates:* the data-provenance practice the project's database should document.

**(A5) Hot-water demand modeling.**
- **Jordan & Vajen (IEA SHC Task 26)** — realistic DHW profiles at 1/6/60-min scales, stochastic Poisson-rectangular-pulse model. *Demonstrates:* the standard synthetic DHW profile generator; the demand model should cite/benchmark this.
- **Edwards, Beausoleil-Morrison & Laperrière (2015),** *Solar Energy* 111:43–52 — 12 measured annual DHW draw profiles at 5-min resolution (4 consumption levels × 3 temporal patterns). *Demonstrates:* empirically measured profiles for solar-thermal simulation.
- **Fuentes, Arce & Salom (2018),** *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews* 81(Part 1):1530–1547 — review finding that "the annual and monthly solar coverage factor was strongly influenced by the DHW profile used, with differences up to 24% between values depending on the tapping profile applied." *Demonstrates:* why the demand model materially affects solar fraction — directly relevant to the project's 54–84% band.

**(A6) Validation methodologies for PCM/TES selection or ranking.**
- **Awan, Ma, Lin, Pandey & Tyagi (2023),** *Journal of Energy Storage* 57:106301 — characteristic-oriented AHP ranking of PCMs *verified via TES simulation*. Verbatim: "The cost-based flexibility potential by using the better-ranked PCM thermal energy storage system was 37.1%, while that for the lower-ranked PCM… was 27.5%… The simulation results were consistent with the ranking results." *Demonstrates:* the ranking → simulation validation loop the project is attempting — the single best methodological benchmark for A6/A7. Note this paper achieved *agreement*; the project's disagreement is itself a diagnostic (supercooling omission).
- Monte Carlo stability + sensitivity analysis for MCDM (Cui et al. 2023, *Information Sciences* 647:119439; Więckowski & Sałabun 2023, *Applied Soft Computing* 148:110915). *Demonstrates:* the sensitivity/uncertainty layer the project already includes.

**(A7) Combined clustering + PCM ranking + physics-validation pipelines — the novelty.**
No single study combines all three for solar water heating. Closest analogs and what each lacks:
- Awan et al. (2023) — ranking + simulation, **no climate clustering.**
- Martínez-Gómez (2025) — climate + ranking, **no clustering, no simulation.**
- Noor et al. (2026), *Sustainability* — PCM + climate + physics-informed ML (720 EnergyPlus runs, 9 PCMs, 10 ASHRAE zones, XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost/RF, R² ≈ 0.98–0.99) but **uses predefined ASHRAE zones and predicts energy, not a PCM rank.**
- K-means-zoning + EnergyPlus papers (e.g., Zeleke, Kumar & Rajasekar 2022, *Frontiers in Sustainable Cities* 4:684148) — clustering + simulation, **no PCM selection.**
*Conclusion:* the project's clustering → ranking → physics-validation loop for PCM/SWH is architecturally unclaimed. (This is absence-of-evidence — a confirmatory Scopus/WoS Boolean search is recommended before asserting novelty in the thesis.)

### PART B — Does GMM + MCDM qualify as "AI-enabled intelligent selection"?

**Verdict: Partially, and it is the weaker end of the term as used in 2022–2026 literature.** GMM is legitimately unsupervised ML, and entropy/CRITIC-weighted MCDM is a recognized decision-science method, but recent papers that claim "AI-enabled intelligent selection" typically add a *supervised* predictor or an *optimization* layer.
- ML + MCDM integration is now the expectation: *Technology in Society* (2025) hybrid ML+MCDM; interpretable-AI + MCDM supplier selection (2024, *Supply Chain Analytics*); Nature *Scientific Reports* (2025) hybrid ML-enhanced MCDM (PCA + k-means + Monte Carlo); MDPI *Mathematics* (2026, 14(1):33) taxonomy of MCDM–ML integration modes. *Demonstrates:* reviewers now expect ML-derived weights, ML property prediction, or ML-validated MCDM — not clustering alone.
- Supervised PCM work is achievable on this data (Part A2). With ~55–62 candidates × several properties and 5 regimes, a defensible move is a Random Forest or XGBoost *classifier* mapping (climate feature vector + PCM properties) → suitability class, trained on labels from the physics simulator (solar-fraction bins), or an ML surrogate that predicts solar fraction and then ranks — converting the negative-Spearman problem into a supervised learning target.

**Most scientifically defensible approach:** a **hybrid ML + MCDM + physics-validation** design — keep GMM for regime discovery (well-justified for probabilistic weather regimes), but (a) add a supervised surrogate (Random Forest/XGBoost) trained on the grey-box simulator's solar-fraction outputs, (b) use MCDM only for interpretable multi-criteria trade-off ranking, and (c) reconcile the two with the physics model. This matches the GA/NSGA-II+MCDM and ML+MCDM hybrids that dominate the recent literature and directly answers the "is it really AI?" critique. Given the small candidate count, prefer tree-ensembles (robust on small data) over deep nets, and report nested cross-validation honestly.

### PART C — Resolving the six specific gaps
1. **Unsourced T_mains = Ta_mean − 2.0.** Replace with the **Kusuda & Achenbach (1965),** *ASHRAE Transactions* 71(1):61–74 undisturbed-ground-temperature harmonic model (adopted by EnergyPlus), which estimates inlet/mains temperature from mean surface temperature, annual amplitude, depth, soil thermal diffusivity, and phase lag. **Burch & Christensen (2007)** "Towards development of an algorithm for mains water temperature" (InterSolar) is the SWH-specific mains-temperature algorithm. *These give a physically grounded, citable replacement.*
2. **GHI quantile-mapping persistence.** **Bailey, Nychka, Sengupta & Bandyopadhyay (2025),** *Solar Energy* 282:113220 — "Adapting quantile mapping to bias correct solar radiation data" (clear-sky-constrained quantile mapping; "RCMs seem to contribute the most to biases and should be corrected for before downstream use"). Also *Energies* (2024) 17(7):1667, ERA5 wind/solar bias correction via quantile–quantile mapping. *Best practice: compute the correction and persist it into the dataset consumed downstream — exactly the step currently missing.*
3. **Salt-hydrate property data.** **Kenisarin & Mahkamov (2016),** *Sol. Energy Mater. Sol. Cells* 145:255–286; plus the Purdue inorganic-salt-hydrate review and the *Applied Sciences* review. *Fills the missing inorganic candidates and documents supercooling magnitudes.*
4. **AHP pairwise elicitation & consistency ratio.** **Saaty (1980/2008)** 1–9 scale, principal-eigenvector priorities, and the **CR ≤ 0.10** rule (CR = CI/RI, RI from Saaty's random-index table); geometric-mean aggregation for multiple experts. *If pairwise elicitation is infeasible, disclose the priors as "expert-assumed" and run a weight-sensitivity analysis — do not label them elicited.*
5. **Supercooling in the physics validator.** Use the **enthalpy method with hysteresis/supercooling,** as implemented in **TRNSYS Type 840** (Graz University of Technology — "takes into account the supercooling and hysteresis that occur with many PCMs") and **Type 860** (Bony & Citherlet, 2007, *Energy and Buildings* 39:1065–1072). *This is the standard way to model supercooling in a tank simulation; adding it lets the validator arbitrate the supercooling-driven MCDM disagreement.*
6. **Physics-based validation of MCDM rankings.** Awan et al. (2023, *J. Energy Storage* 57:106301) is the template. Use Spearman/Kendall rank correlation between MCDM rank and simulated solar fraction, with Monte Carlo error bars on ρ (Curran 2014, arXiv:1411.3816, for Monte Carlo error analysis of Spearman's test). *Present the current negative ρ with confidence intervals and the supercooling explanation.*

### PART D — Objective 2 (DRL control)

**(D1) DRL state/action/reward for PCM-integrated solar thermal / water heating.**
- **Rodríguez et al. (2024),** *Energy* (S0360544224000677) — multi-objective DRL for a water heating system with solar energy and heat recovery; agents trained to deliver hot water, activate a chiller, avoid degradation, and continue service during device failures, with a reward encoding these multiple service goals. *Demonstrates:* the exact multi-goal reward structure this project needs (hot-water service as priority).
- **Amasyali et al.** (cited in Rodríguez et al. 2024) — DRL on a heat-pump + electric-heater water heater trained on price/consumption predictions: "Cost reductions ranging from 19% to 35% were achieved." *Demonstrates:* quantified SWH DRL savings (context-specific).
- **Correa-Jullian, López Droguett & Cardemil (2020),** *Applied Energy* 268:114943 — TRNSYS-simulated solar hot-water system with tabular Q-learning where "20 possible 'action paths' were defined to execute three actions per day." *Demonstrates:* a direct discrete charge/hold action-space analog to charge/discharge/bypass.
- Physics-guided DRL for aquifer TES (2025), *Applied Energy* (S0306261925017143) — DQN/D3QN with domain-knowledge reward to handle delayed rewards and limited data. *Demonstrates:* physics-guided reward shaping for long-horizon thermal balance (relevant given small data).

**(D2) Algorithm comparison (DQN/DDQN/Dueling/PPO/SAC/TD3/DDPG).**
- **Biemann, Scheller, Liu & Huang (2021),** *Applied Energy* 298:117164 — SAC vs PPO vs TRPO vs A2C in a data center: "Soft Actor Critic shows the highest data efficiency and is recommended for applications… can obtain these consistently with up to ten times less data," with ~15% consumption reductions while guaranteeing comfort. *Demonstrates:* SAC's edge for continuous control and sample efficiency.
- *Journal of Building Engineering* (2025) six-algorithm comparison (PPO, SAC, A2C, DDPG, TD3, DQN) vs rule-based — SAC best comfort/energy balance; exponential reward beat linear (temperature fluctuations reduced 1–2 °C). *Demonstrates:* reward-shape sensitivity and algorithm ranking.
- Springer *Artificial Intelligence Review* (2024, 10.1007/s10462-024-10819-x) and arXiv:2401.05737 — benchmark DRL for HVAC, warning that over-use of DQN in continuous problems may hinder progress and recommending TD3/SAC/PPO. *Demonstrates:* discrete-vs-continuous guidance — DQN/Dueling-DQN for discrete charge/discharge/bypass; SAC/TD3/PPO/DDPG for continuous flow-rate control.
- Multi-agent PVT–heat-pump control (2026, S2666546826000583) — Dueling DQN converged faster and higher than PPO (≈10,720 vs ≈9,623 final reward). *Demonstrates:* Dueling-DQN competitiveness for discrete storage control.
- *Guidance:* if action = {charge, discharge, bypass} discrete → **Double/Dueling DQN**; if continuous flow-rate → **SAC** (sample-efficient, stable) or TD3. SAC is the safest default given the sample-efficiency/stability tradeoffs reported.

**(D3) Reward formulations balancing service, utilization, solar use, auxiliary energy.**
- Rodríguez et al. (2024, *Energy*) and Wang et al. (2023), *Energy and Buildings* 301:113696 (RL for ice-based TES) both use weighted negative-cost + comfort/service-violation penalties. *Demonstrates:* the canonical form — reward = −(auxiliary energy) − λ·(hot-water service violation) + μ·(solar/thermal utilization), with service as a hard/heavily-weighted term.
- HVAC studies show exponential penalties reduce temperature violations vs linear. *Demonstrates:* prefer exponential/saturating penalties for the hot-water-availability term.

**(D4) Simulation-environment fidelity in DRL-for-TES.**
- TRNSYS–Python co-simulation for DRL control of TES for classrooms (2025, Elsevier S2352484725004172) — TRNSYS 18 as the environment. *Demonstrates:* high-fidelity environment precedent.
- **TRNSYS Type 840** (Graz) and **Type 860** (Bony & Citherlet 2007, *Energy and Buildings* 39:1065–1072) are the standard PCM/water-tank models (enthalpy method, finite-difference, supercooling/hysteresis). A simplified Python PCM-water model (2022, *Journal of Energy Storage*, Type 7000) checks energy balance each timestep. *Demonstrates:* the fidelity spectrum. This project's grey-box tank (~2 × 10⁻¹³ energy-conservation residual, calibrated to a 54–84% solar-fraction band) is on the low-fidelity end but is defensible for DRL training (fast, energy-conserving); best practice is to **pre-train on the grey-box model, then fine-tune/validate against TRNSYS Type 840/860,** which adds the supercooling physics the grey-box currently omits.

## Recommendations
1. **Immediately (paper-critical):** Reframe the negative Spearman result as a finding driven by the supercooling/validator mismatch, and add supercooling to the grey-box tank via the enthalpy method with hysteresis (TRNSYS Type 840/860 formulation). *Benchmark that changes the recommendation:* re-run the Spearman test after modeling supercooling — a ρ that moves toward positive confirms the diagnosis; if it stays negative, the disagreement lies elsewhere (e.g., latent-heat constraint or feasibility filter) and warrants re-examining the kappa calibration.
2. **Add one supervised ML layer** (Random Forest or XGBoost) mapping (climate features + PCM properties) → simulated solar fraction, trained on the grey-box simulator outputs. This upgrades "AI-enabled" credibility and yields an ML-derived ranking to cross-check MCDM. *Threshold:* report nested cross-validation R²/accuracy; if R² < ~0.7 on so few candidates, keep ML as a surrogate/ranking aid, not a standalone selector.
3. **Fill the data gaps with the canonical citations:** Kusuda–Achenbach (+ Burch & Christensen) for T_mains; persist Bailey et al. (2025) quantile-mapping GHI correction into the Phase-3 dataset; add salt-hydrate candidates from Kenisarin & Mahkamov (2016) and Sharma et al. (2009); document DSC/T-history provenance for all rows.
4. **AHP:** either run a proper pairwise elicitation (CR ≤ 0.10, geometric-mean aggregation, Saaty scale) or explicitly relabel the weights as expert-assumed priors and add a weight-sensitivity analysis.
5. **Externally validate the GMM regimes** against NBC 2016 / ECBC 2017 Indian climate zones (as done with Köppen), citing the *Building and Environment* (2024) India clustering paper as methodological precedent.
6. **Objective 2:** start with Double/Dueling-DQN for discrete charge/discharge/bypass; if moving to continuous flow control, switch to SAC. Reward = −auxiliary energy − λ·(exponential hot-water-service violation) + μ·solar utilization. Pre-train on the grey-box model, validate on TRNSYS Type 840/860.

## Caveats
- Several snippets came from abstracts/aggregators (ResearchGate, ScienceDirect abstract pages); exact volume/page numbers for the most recent 2025–2026 papers (Noor et al. *Sustainability*; the India *Building and Environment* clustering papers, article IDs S0360132324013532 and S0360132325011047) should be verified at the DOI landing page before IEEE formatting.
- The novelty claim ("no study combines all three") is absence-of-evidence from targeted searches; a confirmatory Scopus/Web of Science Boolean search (e.g., `"climate cluster*" AND "phase change material" AND (rank* OR MCDM OR select*) AND (simulat* OR EnergyPlus OR TRNSYS)`) is recommended before asserting it in the thesis.
- Some retrieved items are patents or non-peer-reviewed (PatSnap, USPTO, arXiv preprints); these are flagged and should be treated as supporting context, not primary peer-reviewed evidence. Kusuda & Achenbach (1965), Sharma et al. (2009), Zalba et al. (2003), Abhat (1983), and Bony & Citherlet (2007) are foundational/highly-cited; Martínez-Gómez (2025), Bailey et al. (2025), Awan et al. (2023), Biemann et al. (2021), and Correa-Jullian et al. (2020) are single specific studies cited for a specific technique.
- DRL savings percentages (15–35%) are context-specific to their reported buildings/systems and should not be presented as guaranteed for this SWH application.
- The corrected second author of the DHW-profile review is **Luis Arce** (Fuentes, Arce & Salom 2018), not "Schaefer."
