# 15 — Cluster 0 Bump Chart: Line-by-Line Explanation

Files: `data/plots/tamilnadu_objective1/07_bump_chart_ranks_cluster0.png` /
`.html`. Generated by `p07()` in `plots/generate_tamilnadu_plots.py`, reading
`data/processed/pcm/mcdm_full_scores_by_cluster.csv` (Cluster 0 rows, `n=15`
feasibility survivors). Companion read: `14_PHASE_6_MCDM_RANKING_EXPLAINED.md`
covers the ranking engine in general — this doc drills into exactly this one
chart.

## 1. What the chart is

![cluster 0 bump chart](../../data/plots/tamilnadu_objective1/07_bump_chart_ranks_cluster0.png)

A bump chart with 5 x-axis stops — **TOPSIS, GRA, PROMETHEE, VIKOR, CONSENSUS**
— and one line per PCM, tracking where that PCM's rank sits at each stop. Only
the top-5-by-consensus-rank PCMs in Cluster 0 are drawn (`per_cluster_depth = 5`
in the script), so the chart is a close-up on *who's actually competing for the
podium*, not a picture of the full 15-candidate field. Y-axis is inverted —
rank 1 (best) is at the top.

## 2. Cluster 0 basics

- **Tm target**: 57.0°C (same for every cluster — see doc 14 §5)
- **L_required**: 301.4 kJ/kg
- **Survivors ranked**: 15 (only the top 5 are plotted here)
- **Blended weights**: f_Tm 0.243, latent-heat-margin 0.235, ρH 0.152, TC 0.149, cycling 0.221
- **Kendall's W**: 0.842 (strong four-method agreement — this is *not* a
  cluster where the methods are wildly split; the interesting story is in the
  detail, not the headline number)
- **Borda vs. Copeland**: agree on #1 (`n-Octacosane (C28)`)

## 3. The five lines, in order of the chart's legend

### `n-Octacosane (C28)` — flat line at rank 1

Ranked #1 by **every single method**: TOPSIS, GRA, PROMETHEE, VIKOR, and
therefore Consensus. This is the only PCM in the top-5 with a perfectly flat
line — total four-method agreement.

Why: it's not the best on any one criterion in isolation (its Tm, 61.6°C, is
4.6K off the 57°C target — a mediocre `f_Tm` of 0.516 raw / 0.387 normalized,
the *worst* Tm-fitness of these five candidates). It wins because it's the
strongest **all-round** candidate — highest volumetric latent heat density in
the whole cluster (ρH = 230.2 MJ/m³, normalized 1.000), near-best thermal
conductivity (0.2665 W/mK, normalized 0.840), strong cycling confidence (1,581
tested cycles, normalized 0.675), and a still-solid latent-heat margin
(normalized 0.927). See doc 14 §8 for the full normalized-score table. Every
method's aggregation logic — however differently each one trades off criteria
— converges on the same answer once no criterion is individually weak.

### `n-Hexacosane (C26)` — rank 2 in TOPSIS/GRA/PROMETHEE, drops to rank 3 in VIKOR, back to 2 in Consensus

Melts at 56.5°C — essentially *on* the 57°C target (`f_Tm` normalized = 1.000,
the best Tm-fitness of any candidate in the cluster) — and has the highest raw
latent heat margin too. That's enough to hold #2 in TOPSIS, GRA and PROMETHEE.

It slips to **#3 in VIKOR**, behind `PureTemp 58`, because VIKOR's compromise
score explicitly penalizes a candidate's *single worst* weighted gap (the `R`
term, individual regret) alongside its overall gap (`S`). n-Hexacosane's
cycling confidence is the **second-lowest of the top 5** (1,404 tested cycles,
normalized 0.121) and its ρH is mid-pack (normalized 0.364) — under VIKOR's
regret-sensitive logic, that combination of one fairly weak axis plus one
mediocre axis costs it more than it does under TOPSIS/GRA/PROMETHEE, which
each spread the same weakness more thinly across a Euclidean or grey-relational
aggregate. Consensus (Borda across all 4 ranks) still lands it at #2 because
three-out-of-four methods still call it #2.

### `PureTemp 58` — rank 3 in TOPSIS, drifts to rank 4 in GRA, rank 5 in PROMETHEE, back up to rank 2 in VIKOR, settles at rank 3 in Consensus

The chart's most "zig-zag" line among the top 3, and the clearest illustration
of *why a consensus step is needed at all*. Melting at 58.0°C (`f_Tm`
normalized 0.970 — very close to n-Hexacosane's perfect score) and with the
**best cycling confidence of the top 5** (1,620 tested cycles, normalized
0.789), PureTemp 58 is the candidate VIKOR likes most after n-Octacosane
(rank 2) — no catastrophic single weakness, decent everywhere.

But it has the **weakest thermal conductivity of the top 5** (0.200 W/mK,
normalized 0.194) and a below-median latent-heat margin (normalized 0.244).
PROMETHEE's pairwise outranking is the most sensitive to exactly this kind of
profile: PureTemp 58 loses ground on TC and latent-margin against *several*
peers simultaneously in pairwise comparisons, each contributing to a worse net
flow — pushing it down to rank 5 under PROMETHEE specifically, even though
TOPSIS and VIKOR treat the same underlying numbers far more forgivingly.

### `RT57HC` — rank 4 in TOPSIS, rank 3 in GRA/PROMETHEE, drops to rank 5 in VIKOR, rank 4 in Consensus

Also melts at 56.5°C (identical Tm to n-Hexacosane, so identical `f_Tm` =
1.000 normalized) but with a lower latent-heat margin (normalized 0.610 vs.
n-Hexacosane's 1.000) and a mid-pack ρH (normalized 0.727) — good enough for
GRA/PROMETHEE to place it at #3 (ahead of PureTemp 58), but not enough of an
edge for TOPSIS or VIKOR to rate it above PureTemp 58 or n-Hexacosane. Its dip
to rank 5 under VIKOR mirrors n-Hexacosane's — same 1,404-cycle cycling
confidence (normalized 0.121), which VIKOR's regret term punishes harder.

### `PureTemp 53` — rank 5 in TOPSIS, rank 8 in GRA, rank 9 in PROMETHEE, jumps to rank 4 in VIKOR, rank 5 in Consensus

The chart's steepest, most visually dramatic line — it falls *off the bottom
of the plotted range* under GRA and PROMETHEE (both rank it 8th/9th among all
15 survivors, well outside this top-5 view — which is exactly why its line
looks like it plunges off the chart at those two stops) and then rockets back
up to rank 4 under VIKOR.

Why the swing: PureTemp 53 melts at 53.0°C, 4K off-target — a mediocre `f_Tm`
(normalized 0.503, tied with PureTemp 60) — combined with a **below-median
latent-heat margin** (normalized 0.244, tied for second-worst of the top 5)
and the **weakest thermal conductivity tied with PureTemp 58** (0.200 W/mK,
normalized 0.194). GRA's grey-relational coefficient and PROMETHEE's pairwise
flows both aggregate weaknesses *across every criterion at once against every
peer*, so being mediocre-to-weak on three of five axes compounds badly under
those two methods. But PureTemp 53 has the **single best cycling confidence in
the entire cluster** (1,686 tested cycles, normalized 0.975) — and VIKOR's
compromise formula, built from a *weighted-sum* group-utility term (S) plus a
*single-worst-gap* regret term (R) rather than a full-vector distance or
pairwise comparison, lets that one standout strength offset several
average-to-weak criteria far more than GRA or PROMETHEE allow. Consensus
(Borda, summing points across all 4 methods) ends up putting it at #5 — the
TOPSIS and VIKOR points partially compensate for the GRA/PROMETHEE penalty,
but not enough to beat RT57HC's more consistently mid-pack profile.

## 4. The pattern across all five lines

| PCM | Flattest at | Most volatile at | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| n-Octacosane (C28) | everywhere (rank 1) | — | no weak criterion anywhere — every method agrees |
| n-Hexacosane (C26) | TOPSIS/GRA/PROMETHEE (rank 2) | VIKOR (rank 3) | near-perfect Tm + latent margin, but weak cycling — VIKOR's regret term is what notices |
| PureTemp 58 | — | PROMETHEE (rank 5) vs VIKOR (rank 2) | best cycling of the top 5, but weakest TC — PROMETHEE's pairwise comparisons punish it most |
| RT57HC | GRA/PROMETHEE (rank 3) | VIKOR (rank 5) | same weak-cycling issue as n-Hexacosane, same VIKOR penalty |
| PureTemp 53 | — | GRA/PROMETHEE (rank 8–9) vs VIKOR (rank 4) | best cycling in the cluster, but weak on 3 other axes — GRA/PROMETHEE punish the spread, VIKOR forgives it for the one strength |

**The general rule this chart illustrates**: TOPSIS and VIKOR behave similarly
to each other (both reward "no fatal weakness," Spearman ρ=0.91 between them
statewide — see doc 14 §9, Plot 08), while GRA and PROMETHEE behave similarly
to *each other* (both punish being weak on multiple criteria simultaneously,
ρ=1.00 between them on the top-3 sets statewide) — and the two pairs disagree
with each other (GRA/PROMETHEE ↔ VIKOR ρ=0.61, the lowest pairwise agreement
of any method pair in the whole dataset). Every candidate whose line zig-zags
in this chart does so specifically because it has **one standout strength
paired with multiple average-to-weak criteria** (PureTemp 58's cycling vs. its
weak TC; PureTemp 53's cycling vs. its weak TC *and* latent margin) — that
profile shape is precisely what TOPSIS/VIKOR forgive and GRA/PROMETHEE
penalize. A candidate with **no standout strength and no fatal weakness**
(n-Octacosane) is the only one all four methods agree on unconditionally.

## 5. Monte Carlo stability for these same five PCMs

5,000-draw Monte Carlo (weights redrawn from a Dirichlet centered on the
nominal blend; Tm/latent-heat/TC/ρH perturbed) confirms the deterministic
picture is directionally right but not iron-clad:

| PCM | Deterministic consensus rank | Top-3 inclusion (5000 draws) | Top-1 retention |
|---|---|---|---|
| n-Octacosane (C28) | 1 | **76.8%** | 50.8% |
| n-Hexacosane (C26) | 2 | 36.1% | 10.6% |
| PureTemp 58 | 3 | 39.8% | 11.2% |
| RT57HC | 4 | 24.9% | 5.3% |
| PureTemp 53 | 5 | 34.9% | 8.0% |

n-Octacosane's #1 spot is robust (top-3 in ~3 out of 4 plausible reweightings)
but *not* untouchable — in roughly half of all draws something else takes the
very top spot. Note PureTemp 53's Top-3 inclusion (34.9%) is actually **higher
than RT57HC's** (24.9%) despite RT57HC's better deterministic rank (#4 vs #5)
— under plausible weight perturbation, PureTemp 53's single standout strength
(cycling) gets more chances to matter than RT57HC's more evenly-mediocre
profile, which is consistent with the GRA/PROMETHEE-vs-VIKOR/TOPSIS split
described in §4.

## 6. Takeaway for reading this chart

A flat line = a PCM with no exploitable weakness, and every aggregation
philosophy agrees on it. A steep or "off-the-bottom" swing = a PCM with a
genuinely lopsided profile (one strong axis, several mediocre ones) whose
apparent rank depends heavily on *which kind of trade-off logic* you trust —
distance-to-ideal (TOPSIS), consistency-with-ideal (GRA), pairwise dominance
(PROMETHEE), or worst-case-regret tolerance (VIKOR). The Consensus column
exists specifically to keep any one of those philosophies from silently
deciding the recommendation.
